Is the MedexRegAI hours claim right? A critical review against real consultant data

Short answer: v1 overstated the saving about threefold. The per-task percentages mostly hold, but they apply to tasks where an experienced consultant spends few hours. The ROI is real but modest on simple 510(k)s and strong on complex ones. The biggest gains still to win are features we do not claim yet.

1. Why v1 was too high

  1. Scope. v1 gave the consultant 26 tasks. The consultant does 20; the manufacturer owns 5 (test plan, biocompatibility plan, lab coordination, test-report review, risk file) and one is unclear (the AI-letter response). That removed 13.5 of the 55 claimed hours. On the remaining 20 tasks, v1 claimed 41.5 h; the consultant's real hours support 18.3.
  2. Research is fast for an expert. Our differentiators (predicate search, verification, research library) total 10 h for this consultant with AI, against 28 h in v1. A 60% cut of a small number is a small number.
  3. General AI does not help their drafting. They spend the same hours on sections with and without AI. We cannot claim MedexRegAI drafts faster than general AI until we prove it.
  4. eSTAR assembly was overclaimed. MedexRegAI plans and checks the eSTAR, but the consultant still fills FDA's PDF form. We cut the saving from 50% to 25%.
What still holds. On the tasks we kept, the savings are defensible. The SE table (10 → 6 h), research library (6 → 3.5 h), claim-evidence check (6 → 4 h) and review tracking (6 → 4 h) are all features in the PRD. They sit on tasks where the consultant already sees AI help, which is a good sign. The draft we audited in v1 (two invalid predicate K-numbers) shows that verification protects quality even when it saves only 1.5 h.

Pricing implications

Simple 510(k)s on Starter and ad-hoc are weak. At $1,650 a project, Starter returns 0.7x on a Huber-type project (break-even 11 h). It is still positive, but it is a hard sell to a consultant who saves 18 h. Options: steer consultants to Team or Practice; offer a lower-priced "simple 510(k)" project credit; or make the first year's retainer cheaper for consultancies.
Lead with complex projects and volume. The pump gives 5.4x on Team and 9.4x on Practice. A consultant doing about 10 to 15 projects a year fits Team or Practice. Open conversations with consultancies that do software or connected devices.

5. Risks in the v3 numbers

  • n = 1. One experienced consultant. A junior consultant may save more on research; a faster one may save less on QC.
  • Pump calibration. Technical documentation (130 h) keeps our model hours because the Huber section hours matched our model. If the manufacturer writes most of it, the consultant's pump hours, and the upside, shrink.
  • Pre-launch. Every MedexRegAI hour is a target until the 12-week pilot measures it.
  • Rate. $150/h is conservative. At $250/h every ROI figure rises by about 70%.
