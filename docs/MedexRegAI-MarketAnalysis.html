Executive summary

There is no incumbent that does what MRA-REQ-001 describes. There are four adjacent categories that each do part of it, and a fifth — the consultant — that does all of it by hand at $150–$500 per hour. The opening is real, but it is narrower than it looks and it is closing: five AI-native 510(k) tools have appeared in roughly the last eighteen months, and the eQMS platforms that already own the customer relationship are shipping AI regulatory features through 2026.

The seven findings that matter

F-1The market is barbelled and the middle is empty. AI-native 510(k) tools sell at $790–$3,490 per year. Enterprise regulatory suites sell at $25,000–$200,000. Nothing credible sits between. The 5,527 companies that filed one to four 510(k)s in five years live in that gap.Opportunity
F-2Everyone sells search; nobody sells a determination. Basil Systems has the deepest FDA device data available commercially and still ends at a result list. The pathway engine, confidence-scored product-code shortlist and reasoning chain in MRA-REQ-001 §9 are genuinely unoccupied ground.Wedge
F-3Reproducibility is unclaimed. Several vendors offer source traceability on an alert. None offers an immutable, addressable, diffable snapshot under which a March report regenerates identically in September. Section 6 of the requirements is the strongest structural differentiator in the whole specification, and it is not currently being sold as one.Moat
F-4The 510(k) Summary corpus is the real asset. 94% of recent clearances publish one. Structuring indications-for-use, technological characteristics, testing performed and standards cited — with confidence scores and source spans — is expensive once and cheap forever. It is the part a competitor cannot copy quickly.Moat
F-5Three feature gaps will lose deals in 2026. An MCP / agent interface (Cortellis and Obsidian already ship one), eSTAR-aware section drafting (Complizen and Cruxi already ship one), and a 510(k) change-assessment module (RegDesk sells one). None is in Release 1 as currently specified.Act
F-6Usage is project-shaped, which is a churn problem. A team files, then leaves. The monitoring and alerting features currently sitting in R3 (FR-6.5, FR-11.5) are the retention mechanism and need to move forward.Act
F-7Consultants are the channel, not the enemy. They are high-frequency, quality-literate, reference-generating, and they carry the client relationship into the long tail. Multi-client isolated workspaces (FR-11.2) are currently R2; they should be R1.Act

The one-line thesis. Own the price band nobody serves, with the one property nobody claims: every determination reproducible against a dated snapshot of the public record. Sell it first to the people whose job is to defend the answer — consultants and small RA teams — and let their clients follow.

Method and evidence base

Two evidence streams, deliberately separated, because they carry very different confidence.

Primary — computed, high confidence

Buyer counts, corpus sizes, review-time distributions and market concentration were computed directly against the openFDA API on 24 August 2026, using the same endpoints MRA-REQ-001 §5 specifies for ingestion. These figures are reproducible: the query basis is stated alongside each result. Company counts derive from normalised applicant names (legal-suffix stripped, case-folded), which slightly over-counts where one firm files under several legal entities — treat them as ±10%.

Vendor pricing, market forecasts and funding figures come from vendor pricing pages, procurement aggregators and analyst press releases. Where a vendor does not publish pricing, that is stated as such rather than estimated silently. Six groups compete for this budget. Only one of them — the newest — is aiming at the same target as MRA-REQ-001, and it is aiming with a much thinner data layer.

Group A — FDA data intelligence

Basil Systems is the closest thing to a direct competitor on the data layer, and the one to study. It indexes 510(k), PMA, De Novo, classification, guidance, UDI and clinical-trial data with AI-assisted indexing, updates multiple times daily, and ships an "Ecosystem" view for predicate visualisation. Its own case studies describe transforming weeks of research into minutes — the same claim MedexRegAI makes. Coverage is US-centric and pricing is not published.

What they do not do: issue a determination. Basil helps a competent RA professional find things faster. It does not tell you your pathway, does not model your cost or timeline, does not audit your submission against precedent, and does not produce a citable deliverable. It is a microscope, not a diagnosis. That distinction is the whole opening.

Group B — global regulatory intelligence and RIM

This group sells breadth and enterprise workflow. RegDesk is a Gartner Representative Vendor for both RIM and Regulatory Intelligence, covers 120+ countries, and its AI Application Builder claims submission-ready applications in a week rather than three to six months — with one customer case cutting a Class III timeline from eight months to ten days. Cortellis (Clarivate) brings 300,000+ regulatory reports over 30 years and shipped a Claude integration via MCP in March 2026. IQVIA, ArisGlobal LifeSphere (NavaX agentic AI) and Freyr (200+ markets) are pharma-first with device coverage secondary. Veeva Vault Regulatory is the dominant RIM platform with 450+ customers, but regulatory intelligence is a feature inside a larger submission-management suite with a three-to-twelve-month implementation. Rimsys launched a "Regulatory Execution Engine" in May 2026 and serves 6 of the top 12 global medtech manufacturers. Obsidian is the interesting outlier: a small, focused monitoring product with genuinely published per-user pricing and both API and MCP access.

What they do not do: pre-market strategy for a device that does not exist yet. This group answers "what changed and does it affect my registered portfolio?" MedexRegAI answers "what is my device, legally, and what will it cost me?" Those are different questions with different buyers — but note that Group B is drifting toward us, not away.

Group C — AI-native 510(k) copilots

This is the direct threat and the reason timing matters. Complizen ships predicate analysis, classification, risk intelligence, tests and standards, and a Submission Builder with eSTAR export, at $790 to $3,490 per year with a free tier. Cruxi sells a $300 pathway assessment and a hybrid AI-plus-consultant model at $5,000–$20,000, claiming up to 80% cost reduction. RookAI does AI-driven 510(k)/eSTAR review with expert oversight. Nemedio ($8.5M raised) and Assyro occupy adjacent ground.

Where they are weak: the data layer. These are thin applications over live API calls and general-purpose models. None publishes an accuracy methodology, none offers snapshot reproducibility, none has structured the Summary corpus at scale, and several are consulting practices with software attached rather than the reverse. They will win price-sensitive startups. They will not survive a serious RA professional asking "how do I regenerate this report in six months and prove it is the same analysis?"

Group D — eQMS platforms adding AI

Strategically the most dangerous group, because they already have the budget line. Greenlight Guru has 1,100+ medtech teams, is ISO/IEC 42001 certified for AI governance, has shipped AI Chat over QMS data and AI-assisted regulatory intelligence, and targets Q2 2026 general availability for AI-powered design controls and automated submission-document generation. Ketryx raised a $39M Series B in September 2025 (over $55M total) with former Medtronic CEO Bill Hawkins as an investor, and is building eSTAR automation on top of connected lifecycle evidence.

Where they are weak: centre of gravity. An eQMS documents decisions that have already been made. It is built around the design history file, not around the FDA public record. Greenlight Guru's regulatory intelligence is monitoring, not determination, and its device-corpus depth is nowhere near Basil's or what MRA-REQ-001 §5–7 specifies. The realistic outcome here is partnership, not displacement — see §12.

Pricing context that matters: Greenlight Guru's median contract sits at $43,989 per year (range $20,975–$54,739), and it is raising prices from January 2026 by as much as 100% under a "package separation". That price move opens the mid-market — and MedexRegAI should be in the room when those renewals happen.

Group E — consultancies

The true incumbent. It is also the channel, the validation source, and the pricing anchor all at once. Pure Global is worth watching as a hybrid: flat annual fees from $1,000 covering registration and in-country representation across 13–15 markets, positioned as 80% cheaper than traditional consultants, with free AI regulatory news as a demand-generation loss leader. That is a services business using AI to compress cost — the mirror image of our strategy, and a template for how services firms will respond.

Group F — the free alternative, and the FDA itself

Most pre-market startups today use openFDA plus ChatGPT or Claude. It is free, it is fast, and it hallucinates K-numbers. Every sale in the startup segment is first a sale against "free and probably fine".

Separately, the regulator is now itself an AI user. FDA launched Elsa in June 2025; over 70% of staff have used it, with CDRH among the highest-adopting centres, and CDRH-GPT is in beta for device review. In February 2026 a directive forced Elsa's migration from Anthropic to Google, and reports of the tool citing non-existent studies have been public. Two consequences: AI in regulatory work is now normalised, which helps adoption; and buyers will ask hard questions about model dependency and validation, which the platform must be able to answer (see §10, G-7).

Differentiation — where we actually win

Six defensible positions, ordered by how hard they are for a competitor to copy. The first two are structural and take years to retrofit. The last two are commercial and could be copied in a quarter — so they are worth taking now and not relying on later.

D-1Reproducibility by architecture. An immutable, addressable, diffable corpus snapshot means a report generated in March regenerates identically in September, and the difference between the two can be shown and explained. No competitor offers this. It is not a feature that can be bolted on — it is a property of the ingestion design (MRA-REQ-001 §6), which is precisely why it is defensible. Who it sells to: anyone who has to defend an answer to an auditor, a notified body, an investor or their own management. That is the entire professional segment.Structural
D-2The structured Summary corpus. 94% of clearances publish a Summary PDF; roughly 15,000 in the last five years and 176,000 all time. Turning indications-for-use, technological characteristics, testing performed, standards cited and the substantial-equivalence argument into queryable structured fields — each with a confidence score and a source span — is a one-time expense that compounds forever. Basil indexes these documents; it does not appear to have modelled them. This asset is what makes precedent-derived test plans (FR-4.3/4.4) and gap audits (FR-5.3) possible at all.Structural
D-3Determination, not search. The competitive set stops at a result list and hands the judgement back to the user. MedexRegAI's unit of value is a ranked determination with a reasoning chain, a confidence band, the precedent behind it, and a document you can hand to someone else. Reframing the category from "find things faster" to "decide, defensibly" is the difference between a $3,000 tool and a $20,000 one.Positioning
D-4Calibrated uncertainty on economics. Median 2025 review was 127 days; the 90th percentile was 267. Any tool quoting an average is lying by omission, and a language model asked to estimate a timeline is guessing. Statistical models over real decision dates and MDUFA cohorts, presented as distributions with intervals (FR-7.2, and the hard rule in §8 that quantitative work is not done by an LLM), is a credibility signal no competitor is sending.Technical
D-5The empty price band. Enter at $8,000–$30,000 — above the credit-card tools, below enterprise procurement — and take the 5,527 companies with one to four clearances in five years. Being the only serious option at that price is a real position, and Greenlight Guru's January 2026 price rise makes the timing unusually good.Commercial
D-6Published accuracy as marketing. Section 16 of the requirements lists "publish the accuracy methodology openly" as a risk mitigation. It is better than that: it is the only credible answer to a sceptical RA professional, and no competitor in any of the six groups publishes accuracy numbers. A quarterly public Accuracy Report — top-3 product-code accuracy against a held-out set of 500 clearances, precision@5 on predicates against an expert panel, citation groundedness — converts an internal quality gate into a category-defining marketing asset.Commercial

What is not a differentiator, and should not be sold as one. "AI-powered", "built on FDA public data", "weeks to minutes" and "cites its sources" are claims every vendor in Groups A, B and C already makes verbatim. Basil's own case study language is nearly identical to the requirements document's problem statement. Leading with these makes MedexRegAI sound like the fifth entrant in a crowded category. Lead with D-1 and D-2, which nobody else can say.

Positioning and messaging

Positioning statement

For US medical device and IVD teams making pre-market regulatory decisions, MedexRegAI is the regulatory determination engine that turns FDA's public record into a defensible pathway, predicate set, test plan and cost model — every claim resolving to a source record, every analysis reproducible against a dated snapshot.

Unlike FDA search tools that end at a result list, or enterprise RIM suites built for pharma portfolio monitoring, MedexRegAI produces the artefact you can put in front of a reviewer, an auditor or an investor — and regenerate unchanged six months later.

Category to claim

Do not compete inside "regulatory intelligence" — that category is owned by Group B, is understood to mean monitoring and alerting, and puts MedexRegAI in a comparison it does not want. Claim regulatory determination: the pre-market decision layer that sits before the QMS and beside the consultant. The distinction is simple enough to hold in a sentence and it maps exactly onto the product's actual shape.

  • Metering on reports and analyses, per the requirements' Q4 assumption, is correct — but meter generously. Punitive metering on a research tool destroys the exploration behaviour that creates the habit. Cap the report artefact, not the query.

Blended ACV assumption for §11 and §13: a target mix of 45% Practitioner, 30% Team, 20% Consultancy, 5% Enterprise gives a blended ACV of approximately $18,000. That is the figure used in the SOM model. It is sensitive to the Consultancy mix — every point of Consultancy share moves blended ACV up by roughly $200.

Feature and tooling gaps to close

Thirteen additions the competitive picture argues for. Each is stated with what it defends against and where it belongs in the roadmap in MRA-REQ-001 §14. Six are genuinely new modules; the rest are re-sequencing of things already specified.

Priority 1 — close before general availability

G-1MCP server / agent interface. Cortellis shipped a Claude integration via MCP in March 2026; Obsidian ships MCP on every paid tier. This is moving from differentiator to table stakes inside a year, and it is cheap: the retrieval and dossier services in §6 Stage 7 already have the right shape. Exposing classification, predicate search and product-code dossier as MCP tools also makes MedexRegAI the substrate other people's agents call — a distribution position, not just a feature. Move the public API from R2 to R1 and add MCP alongside it.MustR1
G-2eSTAR-aware section drafting. Open Question Q6 assumes a research report is sufficient and defers submission-format output to Phase 3. The competitive evidence says otherwise: Complizen ships eSTAR export at $3,490/yr, Cruxi drafts eSTAR content, Greenlight Guru targets automated submission-document generation for Q2 2026, and eSTAR v6.1 (effective 16 February 2026) added QMSR-aligned and Real World Evidence sections. This is the single largest feature gap against Group C. Recommend revising Q6: draft eSTAR-structured sections in R2 — populating the template, not filing it. That stays inside the §2 scope boundary while removing the objection.MustR2
G-3Consultant multi-client workspaces to R1. FR-11.2 is currently R2. The consultant is the beachhead (§12) and the Consultancy tier is the highest-margin SKU (§9); shipping the segment's defining requirement a release late costs the channel a full phase. Move FR-11.2 to R1.MustR1
G-4Watchlist and change alerting to R2. FR-6.5 and FR-11.5 sit in R2/R3. Usage of this product is project-shaped — a team files and leaves — so alerting is not a nice-to-have, it is the renewal mechanism. Obsidian sells monitoring alone at $240–$2,400 per year, which shows both the demand and the price ceiling for it standalone. Ship monitoring in R2 as part of Team, never as a standalone SKU.MustR2
G-5Free public product-code explorer. The product-code dossier (§7) is already a precomputed per-code aggregate. Publishing ~1,500 active codes as public, indexable pages — clearance volume, incumbent concentration, typical review time, common standards, event rates — creates 1,500 SEO landing pages that are literally the product's own data structure. No competitor does this at scale. It is the cheapest acquisition channel available and it exists as a by-product of building the platform.MustR1

Priority 2 — build during Release 2

G-6New module M13 — 510(k) change assessment. "Do I need a new 510(k) for this change?" under 21 CFR 807.81(a)(3) is the most frequent recurring regulatory question at any company with a marketed device, and it produces a letter-to-file that must be defensible years later — a perfect fit for snapshot reproducibility. RegDesk sells structured change assessment; nothing in MRA-REQ-001 covers it. This is the feature that converts a project purchase into a subscription.ShouldR2
G-7New module M14 — AI/ML device and SaMD pathway support. 295 AI/ML clearances in 2025 from 221 manufacturers, and a Predetermined Change Control Plan expectation that no product in the competitive set handles well. FR-2.7 only detects SaMD. Add PCCP drafting support, AI/ML-specific guidance mapping, and precedent analysis across the AI/ML clearance cohort. This cohort is the fastest-growing, most AI-receptive segment in the market and it is currently buying nothing purpose-built.ShouldR2
G-8Pre-Submission (Q-Sub) package builder. FR-2.5 recommends a Pre-Sub meeting where appropriate but offers no help writing one. A Q-Sub is a meeting request, not a filing, so it sits comfortably inside the §12 scope boundary — and it is the natural next action after a high-consequence determination. Low risk, high perceived value, directly downstream of a feature already built.ShouldR2
G-9Model-portability requirement (new NFR). FDA's forced migration of Elsa from Anthropic to Google in February 2026 made model dependency a board-level question in this industry. Enterprise buyers and their QA functions will ask. Add an NFR requiring a provider-abstraction layer, a documented model-swap and re-validation procedure, and evaluation-suite re-run as a gate on any model change. §15 already has the evaluation machinery; this makes the guarantee explicit and sellable.ShouldR2
G-10ISO/IEC 42001 on the compliance roadmap. NFR-7 targets SOC 2 Type II within 12 months. Greenlight Guru is already ISO/IEC 42001 certified for AI governance and says so in market. In a domain selling AI to compliance professionals, an AI-management-system certification is becoming the checkbox that gets you through vendor assessment. Add it alongside SOC 2, targeting month 18.ShouldR2

Priority 3 — Release 3 and beyond

G-11EU data residency option. Complizen already offers it on Enterprise. Open Question Q7's assumption of single-region US deployment for Release 1 is right for Release 1 and will block EU enterprise deals in Release 3. Note it as a known Phase 3 dependency rather than discovering it in a deal.CouldR3
G-12Non-US-applicant onboarding. 52% of clearances come from non-US applicants — China alone is 18%. These firms need US pathway determination and have no domestic tool. This requires no new data, only localisation and a distribution partner. Cheap incremental market, worth scoping in Phase 3 alongside EUDAMED.CouldR3
G-13eQMS connectors as a partnership wedge. Already in §13 at R3. Given Group D's strategic position, treat these as commercial instruments rather than integrations: an embed deal with a mid-tier eQMS vendor is a distribution channel into an installed base, and it is a defence against being bundled out of the market.CouldR3

Land and expand

  • Land on predicate discovery. MRA-REQ-001 §14 already sequences this first, and for the right reason: it is the most acutely painful manual task and the clearest proof the data layer works. It is also the fastest demo — describe a device, get ranked predicates with an IFU diff, in under fifteen minutes.
  • Expand into cost and timeline modelling, which brings a second stakeholder (finance, or the founder) into the account and raises the budget ceiling.
  • Retain with monitoring and change assessment (G-4, G-6). This is the difference between a tool bought for a project and a subscription renewed for years, and it is currently the weakest part of the commercial design.

The structural GTM risk, stated plainly. This product is bought for a project and the project ends. Without the monitoring and change-assessment hooks, Year 2 churn will be severe regardless of how good the analysis is — a team that has filed has no reason to open the tool again for eighteen months. Everything in §10 marked G-4 and G-6 is a revenue-retention requirement wearing a feature's clothing, and should be argued for on those terms.
