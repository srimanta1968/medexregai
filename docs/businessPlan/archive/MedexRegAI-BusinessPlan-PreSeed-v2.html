Every competitor in regulatory intelligence prices a seat or a suite, and most will not publish a number at all. We publish a flat price for a finished deliverable — $500 a month for the platform, $500 for a project — give seats away, back the price with measured accuracy, and add a revenue line nobody else in the category has: a consultant lead marketplace fed by our own free tier.

The ask

We are raising $1,000,000 for an 18-month runway. The build plan is unchanged — a US product live at month 6, general availability at month 12. What has changed is how it is sold: from a $8,500–$29,000 annual subscription to $500/month plus $500/project, with free seats and a consultant lead marketplace. That change roughly doubles modelled revenue at month 18 and multiplies paying accounts by seven.

The one change that matters most. Revision 1.0 identified project-shaped churn as the single most under-addressed commercial risk: a team files, then leaves, and an annual seat licence gets cancelled. This revision stops fighting that shape and prices to it. Revenue now tracks usage instead of arguing with it — a customer who goes quiet stops buying projects and keeps a $500 retainer for the alerts, rather than churning to zero.

The pricing thesis

Everyone in this category sells access. We sell answers.

A regulatory affairs professional does not wake up wanting a seat in a regulatory intelligence platform. They wake up with a device and a question: what pathway am I on, what will it cost, who are my predicates, what will the FDA expect me to prove? That is a piece of work with a beginning and an end. Every vendor in the market prices the shelf it sits on; we price the work.

P-1A published price list, in a category that hides its prices. Nine of the sixteen vendors tracked in MRA-MKT-001 §5 publish no pricing at all — “Custom” means a sales conversation, which lengthens their cycle and shortens ours. We publish everything, including the enterprise volume curve, so a 100-project commitment closes on a web page rather than in a procurement cycle.Novel
P-2Priced to the artefact, not the seat. $500 buys a finished, cited, exportable deliverable on a named device. It matches how the work is actually commissioned, it makes the ROI arithmetic trivial against a $10,000–$30,000 consultant research sweep, and it eliminates the annual renewal conversation entirely.Novel
P-3Seats are free. Every competitor charges per user. We charge for work and let the whole team look — the engineer, the founder, the quality lead, the investor. Free seats are how a $500 tool becomes the thing everyone opens, and usage is what produces both the habit and the leads.Novel
P-4A third revenue line that pays for the free tier. The free determination engine identifies, with evidence, exactly which startups are heading into a hard regulatory problem and have no in-house capability. With their explicit consent, those become the highest-intent leads that exist anywhere in this industry — and consultants pay for them. In every competitor’s model the free tier is pure cost. In ours it is a profit centre.Unique

Why $500, twice. It is below every discretionary-spend threshold we have found in this segment — a founder, an RA manager or a consultant can approve it without asking anyone, on a card, in ninety seconds. It is memorable. And the symmetry is doing real work: the platform costs the same as one project, which makes the retainer an obvious purchase for anyone who buys more than one project a year.

Why one price for five different-looking things. Because the marginal cost of all five is the same — roughly $2.40 of inference against a corpus that was extracted once. Charging differently would be charging for perceived value rather than delivered work, which invites negotiation, which is the thing this entire structure exists to avoid. One price is also the only version a buyer can hold in their head, and a price a buyer can hold in their head is a price they act on.

What a project is not

  • It is not a filing. We populate templates; we never submit to a regulator.
  • It is not legal or clinical advice. Output is decision support and requires qualified human review.
  • It is not a consultant. It is the forty to a hundred and twenty hours of research a consultant does before the judgement — which is precisely the part that should not cost $250 an hour.

Four mechanics nobody can copy

A price is easy to copy. These four are not, because each one depends on something structural in the platform that competitors do not have.

M-1The Determination Guarantee. If a qualified regulatory professional reviews a project and finds the determination materially wrong, the credit is refunded automatically — no argument, no support ticket. We can offer this because we measure accuracy against held-out sets and publish the results quarterly. No competitor in any of the six groups publishes accuracy numbers at all, which means none of them can price against their own error rate. This turns the published-accuracy differentiator into a commercial promise.Depends on the evaluation harness
M-2Re-runs are free, forever. Regenerating a project against a newer snapshot costs nothing. You paid for the determination, not for the query. Six months later, one click shows what changed in the public record and what it does to your answer. This is only possible because the corpus is stored as immutable, addressable, diffable snapshots — the moat from §7 of r1.0, converted into a pricing feature. A competitor without snapshots would have to re-charge, and would have nothing to diff against.Depends on snapshot architecture
M-3Credits are transferable across a consultancy’s client workspaces, with per-client usage export. A consultant buys a 30-pack at $350 a project and allocates credits across twelve isolated client workspaces, rebilling each client at their own rate. That moves us out of the consultant’s software budget — scrutinised annually, always under pressure — and into their cost of delivery, which scales with their revenue. It is a completely different and far more durable budget line, and it is why client workspaces are free.Changes which budget we sit in
M-4Free seats, priced work. Every competitor charges per user, which means the buyer restricts access, which means the tool is opened by one person and forgotten. We charge for output and let everyone in — the design engineer, the founder, the quality lead, the investor on the board. Usage produces habit, habit produces projects, and the free tier’s usage produces the leads that fund it. Per-seat pricing would break all three.Feeds the whole model

The Lead Finder

Our free tier tells a startup, with evidence, that they are heading into a hard regulatory problem they cannot solve alone. That is the most qualified consulting lead that exists in this industry — and it is generated as a by-product of a product we were going to build anyway.

How the lead is created

A founder runs a free Pathway Determination. The engine returns a high-consequence flag — Class III territory, De Novo likely, no clean predicate, novel technology, combination product, or a cost and timeline exposure above a threshold. At that moment the platform knows, from evidence rather than from a form:

  • the device, its likely product code, class and pathway;
  • how hard the pathway is, and why, with the precedent behind it;
  • the modelled cost and timeline exposure — so the size of the engagement;
  • that the company has no in-house regulatory capability, because they are on the free tier.

The user is then offered, once and without pressure: “This looks like a pathway where experienced help pays for itself. Would you like us to share your brief with up to three qualified consultants?” If they tick the box — and only then — the brief is released.

Why this is defensible

L-1The lead cannot exist without the determination engine. A lead-generation company has no corpus and cannot qualify anything. A regulatory data vendor has no free tier and therefore no supply. The only way to build this is to have built the thing we are building.Moat
L-2Double opt-in, always. No scraped data, no cold outreach, no inferred intent. The prospect ticks a box and their brief is released — nothing else ever leaves the tenant. In a domain where pre-market device IP is the most sensitive asset a company owns, this is not a compliance detail; it is the only design that survives contact with a customer’s legal team.Trust
L-3It flips the free tier from cost to profit. Every competitor with a free tier is subsidising non-payers. Ours funds itself, which means we can be more generous with it than anyone else can afford to be — which grows the funnel, which grows the leads. That is the flywheel.Compounding
L-4It makes consultants structurally allied to us. MRA-MKT-001 §12 already identifies consultants as the beachhead and the channel rather than the competition. The Lead Finder makes that literal: we send them business, they bring their clients onto the platform, and their client workspaces are free. Both sides of the marketplace are the same relationship.Channel

The guardrail this depends on, and it is absolute. The determination engine must never be biased toward producing a high-consequence flag, because a flag creates a saleable lead. The two systems are separated: the flag is evidence-driven, its logic is published, its rate is reported in the quarterly Accuracy Report, and no revenue signal reaches the model or its prompts. If a buyer ever suspects we inflate difficulty to sell leads, the entire trust proposition — which is the whole company — is gone. This is written into the product specification as a standing gate, not left to good intentions.

The honest cost of this change, stated plainly. Two things get worse. First, the serviceable market shrinks from $195M to $87M — a $500-a-month product simply cannot extract $18,000 of blended value per account, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. Second, the model becomes more fragile at low volume: fifteen customers under r1.0 was $218,000 of revenue; fifteen under r2.0 is $70,000. There is no ACV to make small numbers work and no sales team to force them. The entire model rests on the organic funnel reaching roughly 12,000 sessions a month by month 18.

That is a real bet. It is the right one for this company because the funnel asset — ~1,500 product-code pages — is a by-product of the product itself and costs almost nothing to build, whereas a sales team is $180,000 a head and is not in the budget. But it should be underwritten with eyes open, and §17 sets the month-12 decision point at which we would know.

Recommendation

R-1Adopt r2.0, and do not run both models. A $500/month product and a $22,000/year tier on the same price list confuse the buyer and cannibalise each other. The volume curve already recreates a mid-market and enterprise price point — $4,800, $10,500, $30,000 — arrived at through usage rather than negotiation, which is strictly better.Decide
R-2Publish the volume curve on day one. Without it, $500/month caps the business at roughly $5,500 blended and forecloses the mid-market. With it, an enterprise buyer can spend $66,000 without ever speaking to us. This single page is what keeps the $87M market from being a $40M one.Decide
R-3Instrument the funnel from month 6 and set a hard decision point at month 12. If organic sessions, free-to-paid conversion and project repeat rate are tracking below 60% of plan at month 12, the response is to reprice upward toward the mid-market rather than to spend into a channel that is not working. That decision needs a date on it now, not a discussion later.Decide

The expansion gate, unchanged and non-negotiable. No second jurisdiction opens until the US business shows net revenue retention ≥ 110%, project repeat rate ≥ 2.2 per active account per year, and consultant-sourced accounts ≥ 30%. The temptation at month 12 will be to announce Europe, because it is a better slide than a retention number. Expanding geographically before the first market retains does not diversify a retention problem — it duplicates it.

What expansion means under r2.0 pricing, specifically. Under an annual-subscription model a new jurisdiction means a new tier and a new sale. Under pay-for-answers it is a new SKU on an existing account: a customer already buying US projects at $500 starts buying EU projects at $500. Net revenue retention becomes a function of catalogue breadth rather than of upselling seats — a far easier thing to engineer, and the reason the Year-4 and Year-5 numbers in §15 compound the way they do.

What we are asking for beyond capital

  • Introductions to practising RA consultants with Class II portfolios. They are the beachhead, the validation source, the referral channel and the demand side of the marketplace — four roles in one relationship.
  • Access to medtech portfolio companies for free accounts. At $500 the conversion friction is negligible, and portfolio companies are exactly the pre-market startup segment the model is built for.
  • A hard read on the funnel assumptions in §12. Five conversion rates carry the entire model. We would rather have them challenged now than defended later.

Terms

Instrument and valuation are open. We are indifferent between a priced round and a SAFE and would rather optimise for the right investors than for the last basis point of a cap. We are not flexible on the size: below $1M the go-to-market line gets cut first, and under this model the go-to-market line is the revenue engine.

The one-line thesis. Everyone in regulatory intelligence sells access, hides their prices and charges by the seat. We publish a flat price for a finished answer, give the seats away, guarantee the determination against measured accuracy, and let the free tier pay for itself by introducing the hardest cases to the consultants who want them.
