1. Before anything else: the draft would not survive FDA review as written

We checked every verifiable claim in the draft against the openFDA 510(k), classification, recall and MAUDE databases and against three cleared Huber-needle 510(k) summaries. Both predicate K-numbers fail. That is exactly the error a chatbot cannot catch for you, because it cannot query the FDA record each time it answers.

Why it matters. FDA runs its acceptance review within the first 15 calendar days. Historically 63% to 68% of 510(k)s received a first-cycle Additional Information request (FY2018 to FY2022), and an applicant has 180 days to respond. A non-existent predicate is a defect a reviewer finds immediately. Section 5 quantifies this rework using FDA's own review statistics.

3. Who does what: consultant, general AI, MedexRegAI

General AI is already good at drafting text. It does not save time where the work depends on the FDA record, on project state over five months, or on proving where a claim came from. That is where MedexRegAI is designed to work.

Consultant only
  • Client relationship, scoping, pricing, expectation management
  • Regulatory strategy and pathway judgement; signing off the SE argument
  • Pre-Sub meetings and every interactive call with the FDA reviewer
  • Choosing labs, negotiating protocols, judging test deviations
  • Final accountability for content; the client signs the Truthful & Accuracy statement
General AI does well today
  • First drafts: device description, test summaries, cover letter, 510(k) Summary
  • Summarising uploaded test reports into tables
  • Emails, RFQs, status reports, redline summaries
  • Explaining a standard or guidance in plain language
General AI: verify everything
  • Product code and classification name (the draft mixes PTI and FMI)
  • SE table: well formatted, but predicate facts unverified
  • Biocompatibility endpoints and standard editions
  • Peer-reviewed tests found 3% to 13% hallucinated citation URLs from deep-research agents (arXiv 2604.03173)
Only MedexRegAI (by design)
  • Ranks predicates across the whole 510(k) database with live K-number, recall and MAUDE checks
  • Test plan derived from what cleared predicates actually tested
  • Citation gate: no regulatory claim is output without a source record
  • eSTAR section plan, continuous gap check, claim-evidence matrix
  • AI-letter deficiency extraction and firm-wide precedent search
  • Immutable snapshots, chronology export, multi-client workspaces with rebillable credits

When it is found matters more than whether. Found before filing, it is 35 hours of work.   2. Catches what chat cannot. The audit above found two invalid predicates, a mismatched classification name and outdated biocompatibility standards in a single draft.

  5. You stay in charge. MedexRegAI never files and never signs. It prepares the evidence; you make the judgement calls.

8. Method, assumptions and limits

Verified facts. Product codes, K-numbers, review times, recalls and MAUDE counts come from the openFDA device APIs (data last updated 12 Oct 2026, queried 22 Oct 2026). Predicate details come from the FDA-hosted 510(k) summaries K241353, K252355 and K230715. FY2027 user fees come from FDA's MDUFA fees page.

Modelled estimates. Task hours are Projexlight estimates, not measured timesheets. The no-AI totals were checked against published consultant pricing: $33,900 for the Huber needle falls inside the $18k to $40k band reported for moderate-complexity 510(k)s, and $90,000 for the pump sits at the top of the "high complexity, $40k to $85k+" band. Published hourly rates run from $150 to $450+ (NIH SEED, OMC Medical), so $150/h is a conservative rate. The AI-request hours are an expected value: 65% probability, the midpoint of FDA's 63% to 68% historical first-cycle rate.

The reductions used here follow the PRD targets (predicate research at least 60% less time, research library at least 60%, eSTAR checklist at least 50%, claim cross-check and AI response at least 40%, chronology at least 70%) and will be measured in the consultant pilot against the week-12 gate.
