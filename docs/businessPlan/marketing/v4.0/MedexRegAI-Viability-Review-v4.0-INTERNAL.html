Is MedexRegAI a viable business? Not as planned. Possibly, if we build what consultants actually spend their time on.

A consultant told us the platform is a nice-to-have that does not solve the bigger problem: turning client-submitted information into a submission. Our own time data agrees. The current plan builds FDA research first, which took 10 of 98 hours in that study, and leaves client material, which took 60, until after Series A. Nothing in this repository is built yet (it holds documents and research only), so changing the order mainly costs a planning decision. Viability is still unproven and needs three tests, which can finish by 18 December.

4. How we solve it: client evidence to submission

The product becomes a layer between the firm's existing folders and FDA's eSTAR, with the consultant in charge at the review step. FDA data remains essential as the check on the draft, but it is no longer the headline.

1. ConnectRead-only link to a project folder in Dropbox, Box, SharePoint or Drive, plus forwarded email. No files stored.
2. Read and sortWord, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF (including scans), CSV and drawings, sorted by type, version and eSTAR section.
3. Find problemsMissing items, old versions and contradictions, such as specification vs drawing vs report vs IFU. Sends the client a request with an upload link.
4. DrafteSTAR sections, summary tables and admin documents in Word. Each statement links to its source page and FDA checks run on the draft.
5. Consultant decidesReviews in Word, then fills and files eSTAR as today. We never file or sign. The record exports to the firm's eQMS.

QuestionFinding
Accounts needed to cover costsCurrent spending is about $667K a year ($1M over 18 months). At 85% gross margin and $13,500 average revenue per account (between Team and Practice), break-even is about 58 consultancy accounts (modelled). That is 17% of the 344 firms acting as US agent for five or more establishments, or 5% of the 1,201 acting for two or more. Those counts are proxies, not a census of 510(k) consultancies.
How realistic is that?Hard on consultancies alone. The plan's conservative case reaches 30 by M18. We should expect to need a second buyer: manufacturers who file repeatedly and have the same client-file problem internally (about 1,869 companies with 2–4 clearances and 717 with 5 or more, 2021–2025, from the plan's verified counts).
Delivery costThe plan's $2.40 of AI cost per project will not hold. Reading a whole client package and drafting sections costs an estimated $9 for a simple project and $36 for a complex one at current list prices (1,500 pages assumed for simple, four times that for complex; to be measured). With the plan's other delivery costs, gross margin would be about 86–92% at $500 and 92–95% at $900. OCR, drawings and heavier support could lower it, so the break-even above uses 85%.
Pricing pressureComplizen listed $3,490 a year for its Pro plan when the plan checked in August 2026. Team at $10,800 must beat it on real outcomes, not features. Rebilling helps: $900 is under 6% of a $17,500 engagement, and the workbook now asks whether firms would do this.
CompetitionThis space is not empty. Complizen says it ingests specs and test reports "any format", drafts sections with citations and exports to eSTAR, and sells a done-for-you 510(k) from $11,000, which puts it in competition with consultants. Assyro advertises eSTAR authoring. Both are vendor claims, not verified. We should test whether consultants have tried them and what they found. Our possible edge: built for multi-client consultancies, works with their existing folders, no competing service line, measured acceptance data. None of that is proven yet.
Verdict todayNot yet viable on evidence, and not disproven. The problem looks real and costly, and the money works if the targets hold. Whether we can win is unknown until the tests below report.

7. Long-term outlook: the medical device ecosystem

An outlook, not a commitment. Each stage earns the next by creating data and trust the next stage needs. Timing is indicative. Stages 3 and 4 align with the OEM Exchange and Device Marketplace in business plan r6; all market sizes remain unvalidated.

1

Compliance preparation, filing and approval (now to about M18)

Client evidence to submission for 510(k). AI: reads, checks and drafts. Creates: a structured record linking each device claim to its evidence and FDA outcome.

2

Lab test data capture and refinement (about M12 to M30)

Direct intake from test labs and raw data. AI: spots outliers, protocol deviations and weak sample sizes, compares results with what comparable clearances tested, and flags likely FDA questions before filing. Requires: manufacturer permission, and lab partnerships. Gate: stage 1 retention.

3

Manufacturers, OEMs and suppliers (about M20 to M36)

Verified directory using FDA registration and inspection data; test-plan RFQs; reuse of supplier certificates and sterilisation validations. AI: supplier matching and risk. Gate: enough active devices to give suppliers real demand.

4

Customers: clinics, hospitals and practitioners (about M26 to M48)

Finding buyers and evaluation sites for cleared devices, using regulatory status as the trust signal. AI: matching a device to clinical need and buying group. Gate: a base of newly cleared devices from stages 1 to 3.

5

Deployment and operations (later)

Installed base tracked by UDI: service, complaints, medical device reports, recalls, and the change assessment already in PRD 5. AI: finds field problems early and feeds them back into risk files and the next 510(k). This closes the loop: field data improves the next submission. New duties: customer-owned operational data and stronger security.

6

Patient care data and engagement (later still, and a different company in risk terms)

AI: predicts problems, suggests improvements and engages patients. New duties: HIPAA and patient consent. The prediction software may itself be a regulated medical device: FDA's January 2026 decision-support guidance covers software for health care professionals and sets no new policy for patient-facing tools. It would need a quality system, IEC 62304 lifecycle work, and possibly a predetermined change control plan. Plan it as a separate regulated product line with its own funding.

Three choices today that keep this path open, at little cost

ChoiceWhat to do now
One device recordModel evidence around a stable device identity, ready to take a UDI later, so tests, suppliers, clearances and field data can link to it.
Data rightsNFR-8 (no training on client data) stays the default and is a selling point. The ecosystem depends on data sharing, so add a separate, explicit opt-in per customer from day one. Never make it a condition of purchase.
Where data lives"Registers, not repositories" and NFR-9 (no PHI) stay for stages 1 to 4. Stages 5 and 6 need customer-controlled stores and a separate PHI environment, so keep those boundaries clean in the architecture.
How to use this outlook: use it with investors, after the stage 1 tests. Do not lead with it to consultants. It adds to their worry about scale and could make us look like a future competitor to them. Stage 1 must stand on its own.
