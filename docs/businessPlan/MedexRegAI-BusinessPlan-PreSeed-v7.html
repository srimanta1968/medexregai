The one change that matters most. Revision 1.0 identified project-shaped churn as the single most under-addressed commercial risk: a team files, then leaves, and an annual seat licence gets cancelled. This revision stops fighting that shape and prices to it. Everyone in this category sells access. We sell answers.

A regulatory affairs professional does not wake up wanting a seat in a regulatory intelligence platform. They wake up with a device and a question: what pathway am I on, what will it cost, who are my predicates, what will the FDA expect me to prove? That is a piece of work with a beginning and an end. Every vendor in the market prices the shelf it sits on; we price the work.

Transparency of the entry price is therefore table stakes.   • It is not a filing. We populate templates; we never submit to a regulator.
  • It is not legal or clinical advice. Output is decision support and requires qualified human review.
  • It is not a consultant. It does the reading, sorting, cross-checking and first drafting of the client’s evidence, so that the consultant’s hours go to judgement. The four rules

R-1The First Project is $500, once per account. On a card, no commitment — the default on-ramp from the free tier. It exists so a stranger can buy the product without talking to anyone and without signing up for anything, and the SKU name states the limit so the pricing page never has to.Entry
R-2A second project can be bought ad-hoc at $1,500 — capped at two a year. From the third, a pack and its retainer. The cap does two jobs: it gives the genuine one-or-two-project customer somewhere to go instead of leaving after one purchase, and it keeps the Starter pack alive. Uncapped, five ad-hoc projects would cost less than the 5-pack and Starter would be dominated. Beneath the cap, every pack still carries the $500/month retainer. Pricing the ad-hoc project at $1,500 rather than $1,000 matters: at $1,000 the crossover to a plan is eleven projects a year, which lets small repeat filers — the best mid-tier segment — escape the subscription and drops recurring revenue to 29%. At $1,500 the crossover falls to seven, so anyone with real repeat volume subscribes.Load-bearing
R-3The retainer bills continuously until the next pack starts. It does not stop when a pack is consumed. A customer who burns a 5-pack in three months keeps monitoring, alerts and workspaces running and simply buys the next pack when they need it — at which point the new pack’s project rate applies. Monitoring never lapses, which is the whole point of having it.Continuity
R-4The First Project is optional, not a gate. A consultancy or a mid-market RA function that already knows what it wants buys a pack and a retainer on day one. Nobody is forced through a trial they do not need.No friction

Four mechanics nobody can copy

A price is easy to copy. These four are not, because each one depends on something structural in the platform that competitors do not have.

M-1The Determination Guarantee. If a qualified regulatory professional reviews a project and finds the determination materially wrong, the credit is refunded automatically — no argument, no support ticket. We can offer this because we measure accuracy against held-out sets and publish the results quarterly. No competitor in any of the six groups publishes accuracy numbers at all, which means none of them can price against their own error rate. This turns the published-accuracy differentiator into a commercial promise.Depends on the evaluation harness
M-2Re-runs are free, forever. Regenerating a project against a newer snapshot costs nothing. You paid for the determination, not for the query. Six months later, one click shows what changed in the public record and what it does to your answer. This is only possible because the corpus is stored as immutable, addressable, diffable snapshots — the moat from §7 of r1.0, converted into a pricing feature. A competitor without snapshots would have to re-charge, and would have nothing to diff against.Depends on snapshot architecture
M-3Credits are transferable across a consultancy’s client workspaces, with per-client usage export. A consultant buys a 30-pack at $350 a project and allocates credits across twelve isolated client workspaces, rebilling each client at their own rate. That moves us out of the consultant’s software budget — scrutinised annually, always under pressure — and into their cost of delivery, which scales with their revenue. It is a completely different and far more durable budget line, and it is why client workspaces are free.Changes which budget we sit in
M-4Free seats, priced work. Every competitor charges per user, which means the buyer restricts access, which means the tool is opened by one person and forgotten. We charge for output and let everyone in — the design engineer, the founder, the quality lead, the investor on the board. Usage produces habit, habit produces projects, and the free tier’s usage produces the leads that fund it. Per-seat pricing would break all three.Feeds the whole model

The Lead Finder

r7 update (7 Nov 2026). The Lead Finder is deferred to Phase 5 (M13+), dates reset at the pilot result. It was $95,000 of the r6 conservative month-18 revenue and $199,000 of the plan case. It needs a free tier, which now also opens at M13. Consultancies still get it later as a reason to stay. The design and safeguards below are unchanged.

Our free tier tells a startup, with evidence, that they are heading into a hard regulatory problem they cannot solve alone. That is the most qualified consulting lead that exists in this industry — and it is generated as a by-product of a product we were going to build anyway.

How the lead is created

A founder runs a free Pathway Determination. The engine returns a high-consequence flag — Class III territory, De Novo likely, no clean predicate, novel technology, combination product, or a cost and timeline exposure above a threshold. At that moment the platform knows, from evidence rather than from a form:

  • the device, its likely product code, class and pathway;
  • how hard the pathway is, and why, with the precedent behind it;
  • the modelled cost and timeline exposure — so the size of the engagement;
  • that the company has no in-house regulatory capability, because they are on the free tier.

The user is then offered, once and without pressure: “This looks like a pathway where experienced help pays for itself. Would you like us to share your brief with up to three qualified consultants?” If they tick the box — and only then — the brief is released.

The regulatory constraint — and why it is the moat. Promoting or taking orders for a device that is not yet cleared is restricted in the United States: 21 CFR 812.7 prohibits promotion and commercialisation of investigational devices, and FDA constrains preannouncement of uncleared products. A naive “pre-order” marketplace would be unlawful.

So the pre-clearance side is designed around it: non-binding expressions of interest only — never orders, never pricing, never payment; a prominent “not cleared for marketing in the US” status on every pre-clearance listing, drawn from the live regulatory record rather than self-declared; audience gating to verified healthcare professionals and institutions; clinical evaluation matched under IDE rules rather than as a sales channel; and written regulatory-counsel sign-off before launch, with the policy published.

Anyone can build a device marketplace. Only a regulatory intelligence company can build one that is demonstrably compliant and prove it. A generic marketplace must either avoid pre-clearance devices entirely — losing the most valuable segment — or get it wrong. The constraint is the barrier to entry.

The expansion gate, unchanged and non-negotiable. What expansion means under r2.0 pricing, specifically. Under an annual-subscription model a new jurisdiction means a new tier and a new sale. Under pay-for-answers it is a new SKU on an existing account: a customer already buying US projects at $500 starts buying EU projects at $500. Net revenue retention becomes a function of catalogue breadth rather than of upselling seats — a far easier thing to engineer, and the reason the Year-4 and Year-5 numbers in §15 compound the way they do.

Terms

Instrument and valuation are open. We are indifferent between a priced round and a SAFE and would rather optimise for the right investors than for the last basis point of a cap. We are not flexible on the size: below $1M the go-to-market line gets cut first, and under this model the go-to-market line is the revenue engine.

The one-line thesis. Everyone in regulatory intelligence sells access, hides their prices and charges by the seat. We publish a flat price for a finished answer, give the seats away, guarantee the determination against measured accuracy, and let the free tier pay for itself by introducing the hardest cases to the consultants who want them.
