Executive summary

This plan delivers MedexRegAI on $1,000,000 over 18 months. A working US baseline product ships at month 6, general availability follows at month 12, and the product’s scope then grows from the US into other markets in defined phases. The budget splits $200K design, $650K build, $150K sales and marketing — and the runway is sized so the plan does not depend on revenue arriving on schedule.

How 42–60 weeks of phase duration yields a month 6 baseline. Phase 1 and Phase 2 overlap. Phase 1 workstreams run to week 20 — expert review, design-partner recruitment and design refinement continue while the build is underway — but the architecture baseline and the proven data-source contracts land at weeks 8–12, and those are the only true dependencies the build has. Phase 2 therefore starts at week 9 and reaches the baseline release at week 26. There is no funding gate between the phases in this plan, because the money is raised up front; §17 replaces the funding gate with evidence gates that trigger scope decisions instead.

The three things this plan gets right

1It ships something real at month 6, not month 12. The baseline is deliberately the narrow core — device intake, classification and pathway, predicate discovery and comparison, standards and test plan, and a fully cited Regulatory Strategy Report, all reproducible against a dated snapshot. That is the demo that sells, the artefact that proves the data layer works, and the smallest thing a regulatory affairs professional will pay for.Ship
2It funds the go-to-market, not just the build. $150,000 — 15% of the round — is ring-fenced for demand generation from month 3. A pre-seed that spends 100% on engineering arrives at month 18 with a product and no evidence anyone will buy it, which is the failure mode that kills the Series A.Sell
3It sequences markets rather than promising all of them. US devices and IVDs are the entire baseline. EU groundwork starts at month 13 and only if US net revenue retention clears 110%. UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, China, India and LatAm follow on Series A money, in the order set out in §14 — cheapest-and-nearest first.Expand

What $1.0M does not buy, stated plainly. The fully-costed onshore programme for this scope is $5.0M. This plan reaches the same Release 1 scope for a fifth of that through three levers, each with a real cost: a hybrid onshore-lead / nearshore-pod delivery model (§12), founder- and equity-weighted compensation for the first three roles, and a thinner contingency a thinner contingency — 5.9% against a normal 12–15%mdash; 7.1% against a normal 12a thinner contingency — 5.9% against a normal 12–15%ndash;15%. The consequence is that scope flexibility replaces financial contingency. §16 names exactly what gets cut, and in what order, if the week-12 evidence is worse than modelled.

What the $1M buys

One paragraph of context for readers who have not read MRA-REQ-001. MedexRegAI ingests the public FDA regulatory corpus — clearances, approvals, classifications, registrations, adverse events, recalls, standards and guidance — into a governed, versioned knowledge base, then applies retrieval-grounded AI to produce the artefacts a medical device team needs before it can sell: a pathway determination, a ranked predicate set, a testing plan, a submission gap audit, and a cost and timeline forecast. Every claim resolves to the FDA record it came from, and every analysis is reproducible against a dated snapshot.

The commercial case sits in MRA-MKT-001: a $195M US serviceable market, a price band between $8,500 and $29,000 that no credible competitor occupies, and a differentiator — snapshot reproducibility — that no vendor in the category currently claims. The investor case sits in MRA-BIZ-001. This document costs the build and defends the schedule.

Why 1.6 is called out as critical path. Everything in this business rests on one unproven assumption: that ~90,000 510(k) Summary PDFs of wildly varying age and scan quality can be turned into structured, confidence-scored fields at usable accuracy. If that is false, the product is a search tool and the differentiation in MRA-MKT-001 §7 evaporates. Proving it on 2,000 real documents costs roughly $6,000 of inference and three weeks of engineering, and it happens before a single dollar of the build is committed to that design. This is the single highest-leverage $6,000 in the plan.

The arithmetic this line has to satisfy. $150,000 acquiring ~36 paying customers is a blended CAC of roughly $4,200 — against the MRA-MKT-001 §13 target of under $6,000 for Practitioner and under $18,000 for Team and Consultancy. At a Year-1 blended ACV of $14,500 and ~90% gross margin, that is a CAC payback of about four months. These are founder-led-sales economics and they will compress as the motion moves to inside sales; the Series A model should assume CAC rises toward $6,000–$9,000. It is still a very good number, and the reason is structural: the acquisition channel is a by-product of the product’s own data.

AI cost model and unit economics

AI inference is the line item investors most expect to be frightening and which is, on this workload, comparatively modest. The reason is architectural: the expensive work — extracting 90,000 documents — happens once against immutable source documents and is cached forever, while the per-user work is small and heavily cacheable.

Assumptions and exclusions

Assumptions this budget rests on

  • Founder compensation is below market. $7,500/month for the technical co-founder and $6,500/month for the product and regulatory lead, with the ~$585,000 difference carried in equity over 18 months. This is the largest single assumption in the plan (§12).
  • Hybrid delivery is available and works. Four to five senior nearshore engineers with 4+ hours of daily overlap, recruited or contracted in month 3. A four-week onboarding cost is inside the schedule; a twelve-week one is not.
  • Scope is the baseline plus Release 1 as defined in MRA-REQ-001 — US medical devices and IVDs. Every geography beyond the US is out of this budget except EU groundwork in months 15–18 (§14).
  • All Release 1 data sources remain free public US Government data. Only GMDN/EMDN nomenclature would carry a licence cost, and it is avoided in the baseline.
  • Regulatory expert availability, not cost, is the binding constraint. The panel is budgeted at $52,000 across both phases; whether named people will commit hours is a separate question and it must be answered in month 1.
  • Single-region US cloud deployment. EU data residency is a Series A item, not a pre-seed one.
  • SOC 2 Type II is readiness-only within the 18 months. Controls designed and implemented, evidence collection running, audit initiated after GA. Certification requires an observation period beyond this programme.
  • Model pricing is current as of August 2026 and assumed stable. Historically this pricing has fallen rather than risen, so it is a conservative assumption; and at 3.5% of budget, even a doubling is absorbed by contingency.
  • Revenue is not required to reach month 18. The base case collects $560,000; the plan is solvent to month 18 at zero revenue.
