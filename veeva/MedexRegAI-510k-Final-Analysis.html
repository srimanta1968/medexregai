510(k) Consulting Pilot Analysis

The Veeva Vault RIM demo compared with what our 510(k) consulting customer needs and with our business plan and requirements, plus a list of what is missing.

1Bottom line

  1. The Veeva demo solves a different problem from the customer's.

    It is a pharma RIM system (Enalapril tablets, eCTD, MAA, decentralised procedure, xEVMPD/IDMP) running one post-approval change across 48 registrations in many countries. A 510(k) consultancy works in one FDA market, mostly pre-market, and its biggest cost is research done before anything is written. Copying Veeva would mean building the expensive part and skipping the part that saves the hours.

  2. The customer's two most time-consuming activities are not in the demo at all.

    Finding and evaluating predicates, and organising FDA research and evidence. Veeva has no predicate intelligence. Both are the core of our business plan (PR-2, FR-3.x, FR-10.2 citations, snapshots). The 50% saving can realistically come from here, and building it for this customer is the same work as building the platform.

  3. The other four areas match Veeva's patterns, but our plan scopes them out or defers them.

    Content plan, traceability, FDA questions and versions, and the submission record. Our requirements say we are not a document control system or system of record, defer eSTAR drafting past the Series A, and put the content checklist in Release 2. This is the main decision (§5). The full gap list is in §6.

  4. Recommendation: a 6-month, low-cost pilot with a stop/go gate at week 12.

    Build two releases of intelligence (Predicate, Research), then one release of lightweight workflow registers (Content plan, Traceability, FDA interactions, Submission record). Keep documents in the customer's own storage.

5The scope decision

Pilot areas 5 and 6, and part of 3, push us toward the system of record that MRA-REQ-001 §2 and the business plan rule out. There are three ways to handle that.

Option A

Hold the line

Pilot covers areas 1–2, and area 3 as a checklist only.

For
Stays inside the plan; least build
Against
The 50% target is unlikely. AI-response and rework hours stay manual
Option B · Recommended

Registers, not repositories

Areas 3–6 as lightweight registers: links, hashes, statuses, deficiencies, relationships. Files stay in SharePoint or Drive. No check-in/out, e-signatures, Part 11 validation or eSTAR filling.

For
Covers all six areas; becomes the plan's consultant workspace (FR-11.2, M-3); closes the eSTAR/checklist gap cheaply
Against
Some scope growth; needs a short plan and requirements amendment
Option C

Build a Veeva-lite RIM

Full document management and workflow.

For
Closest to the demo
Against
Months of build, validation burden, and a direct fight with Rimsys, Veeva and Greenlight Guru

Choose B. It is cheaper than C and meets the customer's needs, which A does not. An AI-deficiency history and a claim-to-evidence matrix are also what a consultancy will pay a retainer for, and they feed FR-5.3 (predicting AI requests from precedent).

What keeps the cost low

  • Narrow corpus. Load 510(k) summaries only for the product codes the customer works in. This also tests extraction quality, the plan's top technical risk, on a small set.
  • No document storage. Link to SharePoint or Drive; store metadata and hashes.
  • No eSTAR filling and no submission. The customer uploads to the CDRH Portal; we record what they uploaded.
  • One deployment with client-workspace isolation from day one.
  • The customer's RA staff as evaluators, which is also our design-partner evidence.

Indicative team: about 2 engineers (full-stack, data/AI), a fractional RA expert and a part-time product lead for about 6 months. Refine after the baseline in §8.

8Proving the 50% reduction

The 50% target holds only if it is measured against a baseline and limited to the scoped activities. Expert judgement, test-lab coordination and FDA review time are not reducible by software. The plan cites 40–120 consultant research hours per filing; Complizen publishes a 30–60% workload reduction, so 50% is at the top of that range.

Weeks 0–2: reconstruct hours by activity from timesheets on 2–3 recent 510(k)s.

Quality checks, so speed isn't bought with errors: AI-request deficiencies per submission, technical-screening holds, predicates rejected late, items found missing after submission.

10Changes to our plan if the pilot succeeds

  • Pull forward FR-5.1–5.3 into the month-6 baseline. This closes most of the eSTAR gap against Complizen and Cruxi without building form filling.
  • Add the AI-deficiency register and claim–evidence matrix to the consultant workspace (FR-11.2) as retainer features. They put us in the consultancy's cost of delivery (M-3).
  • Amend REQ §2 to read "a register of submission artefacts, not a document control system".
  • Use the pilot as the "signed design partner" and "extraction proven" milestones in Phase 1.
  • Add FR-13 "Submission Workspace" (G-1 to G-10) to MRA-REQ-001, with the changes in §6.2 and §6.3.
  • Fix now, whatever happens: the document conflicts in §6.5.

11Questions for the customer

  1. Which product codes or device types make up most of your 510(k) work?
  2. Where do your documents live today: SharePoint, Drive, or an eQMS such as Greenlight Guru?
  3. Can you share timesheets or activity estimates from 2–3 past 510(k)s for the baseline?
  4. Can you share past AI letters and responses, anonymised, to seed the deficiency history?
  5. Do you need separate client workspaces from day one?
  6. Who is the RA lead who will accept or reject outputs at the week-12 gate?
