PRD 1: Foundation and Research Core

The data foundation, tenancy and the two research features that carry most of a 510(k) consultant's hours: the Predicate Finder and the Research and Evidence Library.

Pilot areas1 Predicates · 2 Research and evidence
Business planPR-2 Predicate and Comparative Study (baseline M6)
ExitWeek-12 gate G1
ArchitectureMRA-PRD-008

1Purpose and outcome

A consultant describes a device and gets a ranked, cited predicate shortlist with side-by-side comparisons and recall flags. All the FDA research for the project sits in one library next to the client's own evidence. Every fact links to the FDA record and the dated snapshot it came from.

≥ 60%less time on predicate search and SE comparison
≥ 60%less time collecting FDA research
≥ 0.80predicate precision@5 vs expert
100%citation groundedness

4Workflows

4.1Find and evaluate predicates

  1. The consultant opens a client workspace and creates a Device Project from a free-text description, with an optional draft IFU and documents.
  2. The system extracts a structured profile: intended use, population, anatomy, technology, patient contact, sterility, energy, software. It asks for missing attributes that matter.
  3. The consultant edits fields. Every edit is kept as an override.
  4. The system proposes candidate product codes (top 5 with confidence), and the consultant picks or overrides one.
  5. The system returns ranked predicates. Each has an IFU similarity score, technology similarity, recency, and recall or withdrawal flags.
  6. The consultant opens a predicate side-by-side: IFU and technological characteristics, with each difference marked and noted where it may raise new questions.
  7. The consultant saves a predicate set, writes a rationale for each inclusion or rejection, and exports the SE comparison table (DOCX/XLSX).

4.2Build the research library

  1. On saving predicates, the library fills itself: predicate summaries, the regulation, device-specific guidance, recognised standards cited by comparable clearances, and the code's recalls.
  2. The consultant adds items by search, tags them by topic and eSTAR section, and pins key passages.
  3. The client engineer uploads or links evidence: test reports, protocols, design inputs. Each becomes an Evidence Item with type, date, version and hash.
  4. Every library item shows its source, retrieval time and snapshot. Re-running against a newer snapshot shows what changed.

10Exit gate G1 (week 12)

  • Live 510(k) #1: RA-PRED + RA-RES hours ≥ 50% below baseline.
  • Extraction field-level F1 ≥ 0.90 on 300 hand-labelled summaries in pilot codes.
  • Predicate precision@5 ≥ 0.80 against the expert set (50 devices).
  • Product code in top 3 for ≥ 90% of the evaluation set.
  • 100% citation groundedness on all exports.
  • The customer's RA lead accepts ≥ 75% of outputs without material correction.
  • Tenant-isolation tests pass; audit trail complete.

11Risks and open questions

  • Older summaries are scanned images. OCR quality decides the extraction fallback (post-2010 only).
  • Which embedding model? Choose by benchmark on labelled predicate pairs in week 6 (ADR-006).
  • Does the customer want Excel or Word as the main SE table format? Ask in Phase 0.
  • Are linked-file permissions (SharePoint) enough, or do we need a copy for extraction? Default: fetch on demand, store only extracted text and hash.
