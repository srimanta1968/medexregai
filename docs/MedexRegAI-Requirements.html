Purpose and problem statement

A medical device startup in the United States faces a regulatory question before it faces a market question: what is my device, legally, and what will it cost me in time, money and evidence to be allowed to sell it?

Today that answer is assembled by hand. A regulatory affairs professional searches the FDA 510(k) database for similar devices, opens summary PDFs one at a time, reads indications-for-use paragraphs, guesses at a product code, cross-checks the classification regulation, hunts through the recognized consensus standards list, then builds a spreadsheet of timelines and fees. It takes weeks, it is not reproducible, and small and mid-market manufacturers frequently cannot afford it at all — so they either over-engineer, under-comply, or discover a Class III surprise late enough that it kills the programme.

Every input to that work is already public. FDA publishes clearances, approvals, classifications, registrations, unique device identifiers, adverse events, recalls, warning letters, recognized standards, guidance documents and quarterly review-performance statistics — most of it through the openFDA API. The gap is not data availability. The gap is that the data is fragmented across a dozen systems, keyed inconsistently, largely unstructured at the point where it matters most (the narrative PDFs), and requires domain expertise to interpret.

MedexRegAI closes that gap. It ingests the public regulatory corpus into a governed, versioned knowledge base; applies retrieval-grounded AI to interpret it; and produces the specific artefacts a device team needs — a pathway determination, a ranked predicate set, a testing plan, a submission gap audit, a cost and timeline model, and a competitive and market-access picture — each one citing the exact FDA record it came from.

This document defines the requirements for Release 1. It is written to be read by the client’s regulatory and commercial stakeholders as well as by the engineering team, and it deliberately states assumptions and open questions rather than hiding them. Section 17 lists the decisions we need from you.

Product vision and scope

Vision

Give any device team — from a two-person startup to a mid-market manufacturer without a full RA department — the regulatory judgement of an experienced consultant, delivered in minutes, evidenced against primary FDA sources, and refreshed as the regulatory landscape moves.

The baseline — US, delivered within six months

Release 1 is delivered in two steps. The baseline is the subset below, shipped to paying design partners at month 6; it is the narrowest thing a regulatory professional will run a real device through and pay for.

  • Device intake and profiling — describe a device in plain language or structured form; the system builds a regulatory profile.
  • Classification and pathway determination — predicted product code, regulation number, device class, and the applicable premarket pathway (Exempt / 510(k) / De Novo / PMA), with confidence and the evidence behind it.
  • Predicate and competitive analysis — ranked candidate predicates with side-by-side indications-for-use and technological-characteristics comparison, plus the competitive field for the product code.
  • Standards and testing plan — recognized consensus standards and the performance testing typically accepted in that product code, derived from cleared submissions.
  • Report generation — an exportable, fully cited research report suitable for an investor pack, a design review, or a consultant handoff.
  • Reproducibility — every analysis regenerable against an immutable, dated snapshot of the public record, with citation verification enforced as a hard gate.
  • Consultant multi-client workspaces — isolated client workspaces with white-labelled reports, because the consultant is the beachhead channel, not a later segment.

The rest of Release 1 — months 7 to 12

  • Submission readiness audit — a gap analysis of the client’s evidence against what comparable cleared submissions contained.
  • Post-market risk intelligence — recall, adverse-event and warning-letter patterns for the product code, fed back into design risk.
  • Cost, fee and timeline modelling — FDA user fees, review-time expectations from MDUFA performance data, and testing cost ranges, presented as distributions rather than averages.
  • Market and access signals — market entrant density, establishment footprint, and incumbent concentration by product code.
  • Watchlist and change alerting — subscriptions on product codes and companies, with change notification on saved projects. The retention mechanism, not a convenience.
  • Public API and MCP tool server — so that other people’s agents can call the platform as a regulatory substrate.

Explicitly out of scope

  • Filing submissions with any regulator on the user’s behalf. eSTAR-structured section drafting arrives in Phase 3 (month 15) and populates the template — it never files it.
  • Acting as a Quality Management System of record (eQMS), a document control system, or a complaint-handling system.
  • Any clinical function, diagnosis, or treatment recommendation. The platform is a research and decision-support tool for regulatory professionals — see Section 12.
  • Legal advice. Outputs are informational and require qualified human review.
  • Pharma, biologics, food and veterinary regulatory areas.
  • All non-US geographies during the baseline. Every market beyond the US is an expansion phase with its own gate — see the ladder in Section 4 and the phases in Section 14. Nothing outside the US is in scope before month 15.

Architectural consequence. Because every cell above is a committed future cell, the canonical data model in Section 7 is jurisdiction-parameterised from day one: a Submission carries a jurisdiction and pathway type rather than assuming a 510(k), a ProductCode carries a nomenclature system rather than assuming FDA product codes, and a Requirement resolves against a jurisdiction-scoped obligation corpus. Adding EUDAMED, or the TGA ARTG, or the PMDA device database, then becomes an ingestion and mapping exercise rather than a re-model. This is the single most consequential design decision in the specification, and it is made in month one for a market that opens in month fifteen.

The expansion gate. No second jurisdiction opens until the US business demonstrates net revenue retention ≥ 110%, annual logo churn < 12%, and consultant-sourced pipeline ≥ 30% (MRA-PLN-001 §17, gate G-8). A vertical product that expands geographically before it has retained its first market is not expanding — it is carrying a retention problem into a second market.

Access constraints we must design around. The openFDA API is rate limited (substantially higher with a registered API key than without), so bulk download files — not per-record API calls — must be the primary ingestion route, with the API reserved for incremental deltas and live lookups. The De Novo database, recognized standards list, MDUFA reports and warning letters have no clean JSON API and require export parsing or structured extraction. Exact current rate limits and file layouts are to be confirmed against FDA documentation at build time; they change without notice, so the ingestion layer must treat every source contract as versioned and monitored (see NFR-11).

Data architecture

The platform is a governed knowledge base first and an application second. Regulatory users will not trust an answer they cannot trace, so provenance is a structural property of the pipeline rather than a feature bolted on at the end.

Ingestion and refinement pipeline

Stage 1AcquireScheduled connectors per source. Bulk files preferred; API deltas for freshness. Every fetch stamped with source URL, retrieval time and checksum.
Stage 2Land — rawImmutable object storage. Original bytes kept forever, partitioned by source and snapshot date. Nothing is overwritten; corrections arrive as new snapshots.
Stage 3NormaliseParse, type, deduplicate, resolve entities. Company-name resolution, product-code validation, date normalisation. Rejects quarantined with reasons, never dropped silently.
Stage 4EnrichPDF text extraction and section segmentation; LLM structured extraction of IFU, predicates, standards and testing; classification labels; entity linking across sources.
Stage 5CurateThe canonical relational model plus the predicate graph. This is the single source of truth the application queries.
Stage 6IndexLexical index for exact codes and identifiers; vector index over narrative text; graph index for predicate lineage. Rebuilt per snapshot, versioned alongside it.
Stage 7ServeRetrieval and reasoning services behind the application API, with every response carrying the record IDs and snapshot version it was computed from.

Provenance and versioning — the non-negotiable

  • Every curated record carries source_system, source_record_id, source_url, retrieved_at and snapshot_id.
  • Every AI-derived field additionally carries the model, prompt version, extraction confidence and the exact source span it was derived from.
  • Snapshots are immutable and addressable. A report generated in March can be regenerated identically in September, and the difference between the two can be diffed and explained.
  • No answer is ever served from a derived field without the ability to walk back to the primary FDA record. This is what makes the output defensible in front of a notified body, an auditor, or an investor.

Two derived structures sit on top and are rebuilt with each snapshot: the predicate graph (a directed acyclic graph of 510(k)-to-predicate citations, enabling lineage queries such as “every device descended from this original clearance”), and the product-code dossier (a precomputed per-code aggregate of clearance volume, incumbent concentration, typical review time, standards commonly cited, and post-market event rates — the object most user-facing screens read from).

AI architecture and models

The system is retrieval-grounded by design. A language model is never asked to recall a regulatory fact from its training data; it is asked to reason over records fetched from the knowledge base and to cite them. This is the single most important design decision in the platform, because a fabricated predicate or an invented CFR citation is worse than no answer at all.

Layers

LayerApproachNotes
RetrievalHybrid: BM25 lexical for exact identifiers (product codes, K-numbers, CFR citations) fused with dense vector search over narrative text, followed by a cross-encoder rerankerPure vector search fails badly on codes — DQK and DQO embed almost identically but are different devices. Lexical precision is mandatory in this domain.
Structured extractionSchema-constrained LLM extraction from 510(k) summaries, De Novo orders and guidance PDFs, with validation against controlled vocabulariesEvery extracted field carries a confidence score and the source text span. Low-confidence extractions route to human review rather than into the graph.
ClassificationHybrid retrieval-plus-LLM over the classification database, with a supervised reranker trained on historical device-to-product-code assignmentsRoughly six thousand product codes makes this a fine-grained problem. The target is a ranked shortlist with rationale, not a single opaque label.
Similarity & matchingEmbedding similarity over indications-for-use and technological-characteristics text, constrained by product code and classPowers predicate ranking and competitive set construction.
Quantitative modellingConventional statistics and gradient-boosted models over historical decision dates and MDUFA cohorts — not LLMsReview-time and clearance-likelihood estimates must be calibrated and explainable. Confidence intervals are shown, point estimates are not shown alone.
Reasoning & synthesisAgentic workflow: plan → retrieve → analyse → draft → self-check citations → assemble reportEach step is logged and replayable. The citation self-check is a hard gate, not advice.
GuardrailsCitation verification against the knowledge base, refusal on insufficient evidence, scope limiter, mandatory disclaimer, human-review checkpointsAn unsupported claim is stripped and flagged rather than softened with hedging language.

Model selection

We propose Anthropic’s Claude family, tiered by task so that cost tracks difficulty rather than volume. The tiering below is a starting position to be validated against the evaluation set described in Section 15.

TierModelApplied to
DeepClaude Opus 5Final report synthesis, substantial-equivalence reasoning, submission gap audit, design-improvement analysis — the judgement-heavy, low-volume, high-stakes work
StandardClaude Sonnet 5Predicate comparison, standards mapping, section drafting, conversational Q&A over the corpus — the working majority of requests
BulkClaude Haiku 4.5High-volume pipeline extraction across hundreds of thousands of documents, classification pre-filtering, summarisation, routing
EmbeddingsDomain-tuned embedding modelVector index over IFU text, guidance passages, recall narratives. Selected by benchmark on a labelled predicate-pair set, then fine-tuned on device text if the benchmark justifies it.

Hard rule. Any factual assertion in a generated report — a product code, a regulation number, a predicate, a standard, a review time, a recall — must resolve to a record in the knowledge base. Claims that fail citation verification are removed before the user sees them, and the removal is logged. There is no configuration that disables this.

Functional requirements

Requirements are grouped by module and identified as FR-<module>.<n> for traceability into design, test and acceptance. Priority follows MoSCoW; the release tag indicates the target release.

M1

Device intake and profiling

FR-1.1A user shall be able to create a device project by describing the device in free text, and the system shall extract a structured profile: intended use, target population, anatomical site, technology, patient contact, sterility, energy source, and software presence.MustR1
FR-1.2The extracted profile shall be editable field by field, and every user edit shall be retained as an override that takes precedence over subsequent re-extraction.MustR1
FR-1.3The system shall accept supporting documents (design inputs, draft IFU, test reports, prior correspondence) and incorporate their content into the profile and downstream analysis.ShouldR1
FR-1.4The system shall prompt for missing attributes that materially change classification — for example sterility, invasiveness, duration of contact, or software function — rather than silently assuming defaults.MustR1
FR-1.5A project shall support multiple device variants (models, configurations, sizes) under one regulatory analysis.CouldR2
M2

Classification and pathway engine

FR-2.1The system shall return a ranked shortlist of candidate product codes (minimum five) with confidence score, device class, regulation number, review panel, and exemption flags.MustR1
FR-2.2For each candidate the system shall present the reasoning: which attributes of the device matched which parts of the classification definition, with the definition text shown.MustR1
FR-2.3The system shall determine the applicable premarket pathway — 510(k)-exempt, 510(k), De Novo, PMA or HDE — and state the specific basis for the determination.MustR1
FR-2.4Where a device is 510(k)-exempt, the system shall state the limitations of exemption that still apply and the general controls that remain in force.MustR1
FR-2.5The system shall flag high-consequence determinations — Class III, implantable, life-sustaining, novel technology with no viable predicate — prominently and recommend a Pre-Submission meeting where appropriate.MustR1
FR-2.6A user shall be able to override the selected product code, and all downstream analysis shall recompute against the override while recording that a manual override was applied.MustR1
FR-2.7Where the device may be a combination product or software as a medical device, the system shall detect this and surface the additional regulatory considerations that follow.ShouldR2
M3

Predicate and competitive intelligence

FR-3.1The system shall return ranked candidate predicates for a 510(k) pathway, scored on indications-for-use similarity, technological-characteristics similarity, and recency of clearance.MustR1
FR-3.2For any selected predicate the system shall render a side-by-side comparison of indications for use and technological characteristics, marking each difference and noting whether it raises new questions of safety or effectiveness.MustR1
FR-3.3The system shall exclude or clearly flag predicates that have been recalled, withdrawn, or associated with a Class I recall, and shall never present such a device as a clean recommendation.MustR1
FR-3.4The system shall display the predicate lineage graph for a chosen predicate, showing what it in turn relied on.ShouldR2
FR-3.5The system shall produce a competitive landscape for the product code: active manufacturers, clearance volume over time, entrant concentration, and recent market entrants.MustR1
FR-3.6The system shall support attribute-level comparison against marketed devices using UDI/GUDID data — sterilization method, MRI compatibility, single-use, package configuration.ShouldR2
FR-3.7A user shall be able to save a predicate set to the project and annotate each entry with their own rationale for inclusion or rejection.ShouldR1
M4

Standards and testing plan

FR-4.1The system shall generate an applicable standards list from FDA-recognized consensus standards, filtered by device attributes and by what comparable cleared submissions actually cited.MustR1
FR-4.2Each standard shall show its recognition status, extent of recognition and transition dates, and shall warn where a recognition is being superseded.MustR1
FR-4.3The system shall propose a performance testing plan — bench, biocompatibility, electrical safety and EMC, software, sterilization and shelf life, usability, and clinical where indicated — derived from precedent in the product code.MustR1
FR-4.4Each proposed test shall cite how many comparable cleared submissions included it, so the user can distinguish an expectation from an option.ShouldR1
FR-4.5The system shall surface device-specific FDA guidance documents applicable to the product code and extract the testing expectations they state.MustR1
M5

Submission readiness audit

FR-5.1The system shall present a submission content checklist for the determined pathway, aligned to the applicable CFR requirements and current FDA submission format.MustR2
FR-5.2A user shall be able to mark each checklist item as complete, in progress or not started, and attach supporting evidence.MustR2
FR-5.3The system shall produce a gap analysis ranking missing items by the likelihood that their absence triggers an Additional Information request, based on precedent patterns.ShouldR2
FR-5.4The system shall identify quality-system obligations that apply to the device — design controls, risk management, and the QMSR-aligned requirements now in force — and flag which are prerequisites to submission.ShouldR2
FR-5.5The system shall not represent a completed checklist as submission approval or clearance likelihood; readiness scoring shall be presented as evidence coverage only.MustR2
M6

Post-market risk intelligence

FR-6.1The system shall summarise adverse event patterns for the product code: dominant device problem codes, patient problem codes, and event-type distribution over time.MustR2
FR-6.2The system shall summarise recall history for the product code with recall class, stated reason and root cause, clustered into recurring failure themes.MustR2
FR-6.3Post-market findings shall be presented as design-risk inputs that map onto hazard categories usable in the client’s risk management file.ShouldR2
FR-6.4The system shall state the known limitations of MAUDE — voluntary and inconsistent reporting, no denominator, duplicate reports — wherever event counts are displayed.MustR2
FR-6.5A user shall be able to subscribe to alerts for new recalls, adverse-event spikes or warning letters affecting their product codes or named competitors.ShouldR3
M7

Cost, fee and timeline model

FR-7.1The system shall calculate FDA user fees for the determined pathway using the current fiscal-year schedule, including the small-business reduced rate where the user qualifies.MustR2
FR-7.2The system shall forecast review duration from historical decision data for the product code and submission type, expressed as a distribution with a confidence interval — never as a bare point estimate.MustR2
FR-7.3The system shall produce a total programme cost range combining fees, testing, consulting and quality-system costs, with each component’s basis and assumptions stated and editable.ShouldR2
FR-7.4The system shall model the cost and timeline delta between pathways where more than one pathway is plausible, so the trade-off is explicit.ShouldR2
FR-7.5The system shall generate an indicative programme timeline from design freeze through testing, submission, review and clearance, with dependencies shown.CouldR3
M8

Market access and demand signals

FR-8.1The system shall report market entrant density and concentration per product code as an indicator of competitive saturation.ShouldR2
FR-8.2The system shall map the manufacturing and supplier footprint for the product code from establishment registration data, including domestic versus offshore distribution.ShouldR2
FR-8.3The system shall identify the reimbursement pathway — applicable HCPCS/CPT coding and coverage posture — where such data is available for the device category.CouldR3
FR-8.4The system shall indicate likely care settings for the device (hospital, ambulatory surgery, physician office, home use) and note the regulatory implications of each, particularly for OTC or home-use claims.CouldR3
M9

Design improvement advisory

FR-9.1The system shall identify design attributes that change regulatory burden — sterility, invasiveness, contact duration, energy source, software autonomy — and quantify the pathway impact of changing each.ShouldR3
FR-9.2The system shall recommend design changes that mitigate known field failures for the product code, each tied to the recall or adverse-event evidence that motivated it.CouldR3
FR-9.3The system shall highlight feature gaps against the competitive set derived from UDI attributes and cleared indications.CouldR3
FR-9.4All design advice shall be labelled as evidence-derived suggestion, not engineering direction, and shall show the evidence inline.MustR3

Report studio and export

FR-10.1The system shall generate a Regulatory Strategy Report covering classification, pathway, predicates, standards and testing, timeline and cost, competitive landscape and risks.MustR1
FR-10.2Every factual claim in a report shall carry an inline citation resolving to the FDA record and snapshot it came from.MustR1
FR-10.3Reports shall export to PDF, DOCX and HTML with the client’s branding applied.MustR1
FR-10.4Every report shall be versioned and immutable once issued, stamped with the data snapshot and model versions used, and reproducible on demand.MustR1
FR-10.5A user shall be able to edit generated text before export, with edited passages visibly distinguished from generated passages in the audit record.ShouldR2
FR-10.6The system shall offer focused report types in addition to the full report: predicate analysis, competitive landscape, testing plan, and an investor-facing regulatory summary.ShouldR2
FR-10.7Every report shall carry the scope and limitations statement defined in Section 12, non-removable.MustR1
M11

Workspace, traceability and audit

FR-11.1The system shall support organisations, workspaces and projects with role-based access: owner, editor, reviewer, read-only.MustR1
FR-11.2Consultants shall be able to hold multiple client workspaces under one account with strict data isolation between them.MustR2
FR-11.3The system shall maintain a complete audit trail: who ran what analysis, when, against which snapshot, with which inputs, and what the system returned.MustR1
FR-11.4Users shall be able to comment on and approve analysis outputs, creating a reviewed-by record on the artefact.ShouldR2
FR-11.5The system shall notify a project when underlying data changes materially — a cited predicate is recalled, a standard’s recognition lapses, a new guidance is issued for the product code.ShouldR2
FR-11.6A user shall be able to export all their project data in a machine-readable format, and to request deletion of a workspace.MustR1
M12

Data platform administration

FR-12.1Operators shall have a pipeline console showing per-source ingestion status, last successful run, record counts, and delta from the prior snapshot.MustR1
FR-12.2The pipeline shall detect and alert on source schema drift and halt promotion of a snapshot that fails validation rather than publishing degraded data.MustR1
FR-12.3Operators shall be able to reprocess any snapshot from raw storage without re-fetching from FDA.MustR1
FR-12.4The system shall provide a human review queue for low-confidence AI extractions, and accepted corrections shall become training and evaluation data.ShouldR2

Non-functional requirements

NFR-1Interactive latency. Search, classification and predicate lookup shall return within 3 seconds at the 95th percentile. Full report generation may run asynchronously with progress feedback and shall complete within 10 minutes at the 95th percentile.Must
NFR-2Availability. 99.5% monthly uptime for the application. Ingestion is decoupled from serving: a pipeline failure degrades data freshness, never availability.Must
NFR-3Scale. The platform shall handle the full US device corpus — on the order of hundreds of thousands of clearances and approvals, millions of adverse-event records, and the associated document set — with headroom for Tier 2 and Tier 3 sources.Must
NFR-4Data freshness. Weekly sources shall be no more than 7 days stale; monthly sources no more than 35 days. The freshness of every source shall be visible in the UI, not buried in an admin console.Must
NFR-5Traceability. Every displayed fact shall resolve to a source record and snapshot in one click. This is a product requirement, not an engineering nicety.Must
NFR-6Reproducibility. Re-running an analysis against the same snapshot with the same inputs shall produce materially equivalent output; any variation shall be attributable to a logged model or prompt version change.Must
NFR-7Security. TLS in transit, encryption at rest, tenant-scoped access enforced at the data layer, secrets in a managed vault, least-privilege service roles, dependency and container scanning in CI. Target SOC 2 Type II readiness within 12 months of launch.Must
NFR-8Confidentiality of client input. A user’s device descriptions and uploaded documents are commercially sensitive pre-market IP. They shall never be used to train shared models, never leak across tenants, and shall be excluded from any cross-customer analytics by architecture rather than by policy.Must
NFR-9No PHI by design. The platform shall not solicit or store protected health information. Uploads shall be scanned for inadvertent PHI and the user warned. HIPAA obligations are avoided by scope, not managed.Must
NFR-10Explainability. No AI-derived recommendation shall be presented without its evidence and confidence. Confidence shall be expressed in terms a regulatory professional can act on, not as a bare numeric score.Must
NFR-11Source resilience. Each connector shall assert an expected schema and alert on drift. FDA changes formats and endpoints without notice; the platform shall fail loudly and continue serving the last valid snapshot.Must
NFR-12Cost control. Model spend shall be tracked per tenant and per operation, with caching, tiered model routing and batch processing used to hold marginal cost per report within the target unit economics.Should
NFR-13Accessibility. WCAG 2.1 AA: keyboard operability, visible focus, contrast compliance in both light and dark themes, and screen-reader-correct tables and status messages.Must
NFR-14Data retention. Client project data retained for the contracted term and deleted within 30 days of request. Ingested FDA data, being public, is retained indefinitely to preserve reproducibility.Must
NFR-15Observability. Structured logging, distributed tracing across the retrieval and reasoning chain, and per-stage quality metrics. When an answer is wrong, the team must be able to see exactly which stage produced the error.Must

Compliance posture

The platform operates in a regulated domain without itself being a regulated product. That boundary must be held deliberately, and it constrains both features and language.

QuestionPositionWhat follows for the build
Is MedexRegAI a medical device?No. It supports regulatory and business decision-making by device manufacturers. It has no clinical function and makes no diagnostic or treatment claim about any patient.Never add a feature that interprets patient data or informs clinical care. Any such request re-opens this determination and requires regulatory counsel before scoping.
Does it give legal advice?No. It surfaces public regulatory information and precedent analysis. Determinations require qualified human review.A non-removable scope-and-limitations statement on every report and every determination screen (FR-10.7).

Delivery roadmap

The roadmap has one fixed point: the US baseline product is complete within six months. Everything before it is sequenced to protect that date, and everything after it is sequenced to grow scope outward from the US into other markets, one gated phase at a time. Each phase ends with something demonstrable to a real regulatory user, because the highest project risk is not engineering difficulty — it is building analysis that an RA professional does not trust.

What the baseline deliberately leaves out. Recommended sequencing note. Phase 1b deliberately ships predicate discovery before submission auditing. Predicate search is the single most painful manual task for the target user and the clearest demonstration that the data layer works; it earns the credibility needed for the higher-stakes features that follow. For the same reason, US depth (Phase 3) precedes every new geography: the same buyer paying more is cheaper to win than a new buyer paying the same, and net revenue retention is the metric the next funding round is priced on.
