The problem

A medical device startup faces a regulatory question before it faces a market question. Today that answer is assembled by hand, it costs tens of thousands of dollars, it takes weeks, and it is not reproducible.

What actually happens today

A regulatory affairs professional searches the FDA 510(k) database for similar devices. They open summary PDFs one at a time. They read indications-for-use paragraphs. They guess at a product code, cross-check the classification regulation, hunt through the recognized consensus standards list, then build a spreadsheet of timelines and fees. It takes 40 to 120 billable hours of a single submission. At $250 per hour that is $10,000 to $30,000 of displaced spend per device.

Every input to that work is already public. FDA publishes clearances, approvals, classifications, registrations, unique device identifiers, adverse events, recalls, warning letters, recognized standards, guidance documents and quarterly review statistics — most of it through a free API. The gap is not data availability. The gap is that the data is fragmented across a dozen systems, keyed inconsistently, and largely unstructured at the exact point where it matters most: the narrative PDFs.

And the free alternative is dangerous. Most pre-market startups today use the free FDA API plus a general-purpose chatbot. It is fast, it costs nothing, and it fabricates K-numbers and CFR citations. FDA’s own internal AI tool has been publicly reported citing studies that do not exist. That is the failure mode our architecture exists to prevent, and it is also our clearest sales argument.

The product

Describe a device in plain language. Get back a pathway determination, a ranked predicate set with an indications-for-use diff, a precedent-derived test plan, and an exportable report in which every single claim resolves to the FDA record it came from — regenerable identically six months later.

The rules the architecture enforces

  • Retrieval-only grounding. The model may not assert anything it cannot resolve to a record in the knowledge base. Unverifiable claims are stripped and logged, never softened.
  • Citation verification as a hard release gate. 100% citation groundedness, checked automatically on every generated report. Not a quality aspiration — a gate that blocks the artefact.
  • Quantitative work is never done by a language model. Review-time forecasts come from statistical models over real decision dates and MDUFA cohorts, presented as distributions with intervals. The 2025 median review was 127 days; the 90th percentile was 267. Any tool quoting an average is lying by omission.
  • Ranked candidates, never a single answer. With mandatory evidence display, explicit confidence, and high-consequence flagging. The product accelerates a professional’s judgement; it never replaces it.
  • Immutable, addressable, diffable snapshots. Every analysis carries the snapshot it ran against. Two reports can be diffed and the difference explained.

What we deliberately do not build. We do not file submissions. We are not a quality management system of record. We give no clinical or legal advice. Those boundaries are not modesty — they are what keeps the product outside the regulatory perimeter that would otherwise apply to it, and they are written into the specification as a standing gate on the roadmap.

The moat

Six defensible positions, ordered by how hard they are to copy. The first two are structural and take years to retrofit. The last two are commercial and could be copied in a quarter — which is why we take them now rather than relying on them later.

D-1Reproducibility by architecture. An immutable, addressable, diffable corpus snapshot means a report generated in March regenerates identically in September, and the difference between the two can be shown and explained. No competitor offers this. It is not a feature that can be bolted on — it is a property of the ingestion design, which is precisely why it is defensible. It sells to anyone who has to defend an answer to an auditor, a notified body, an investor or their own management. That is the entire professional segment.Structural
D-2The structured Summary corpus. 94% of clearances publish a Summary PDF — roughly 15,000 in five years and 176,000 all time. Turning indications-for-use, technological characteristics, testing performed, standards cited and the substantial-equivalence argument into queryable structured fields, each with a confidence score and a source span, is a one-time expense that compounds forever. Competitors index these documents; none appears to have modelled them. This asset is what makes precedent-derived test plans and gap audits possible at all.Structural
D-3Determination, not search. The competitive set stops at a result list and hands the judgement back. Our unit of value is a ranked determination with a reasoning chain, a confidence band, the precedent behind it, and a document you can hand to someone else. Reframing the category from “find things faster” to “decide, defensibly” is the difference between a $3,000 tool and a $22,000 one.Positioning
D-4Calibrated uncertainty on economics. The 2025 median review was 127 days; the 90th percentile was 267. Any tool quoting an average is lying by omission, and a language model asked to estimate a timeline is guessing. Statistical models over real decision dates, presented as distributions with intervals, is a credibility signal no competitor is sending.Technical
D-5The empty price band. We enter at $8,500–$29,000 — above the credit-card tools, below enterprise procurement — and take the 5,527 companies with one to four clearances in five years. Being the only serious option at that price is a real position, and the incumbent eQMS price rise makes the timing unusually good.Commercial
D-6Published accuracy as marketing. A quarterly public Accuracy Report — top-3 product-code accuracy against a held-out set, precision@5 on predicates against an expert panel, citation groundedness — converts an internal quality gate into a category-defining marketing asset. No competitor in any of the six groups publishes accuracy numbers. None can respond without publishing numbers they may not want to publish.Commercial

What is not a differentiator, and will not be sold as one. “AI-powered”, “built on FDA public data”, “weeks to minutes” and “cites its sources” are claims every vendor in the category already makes, nearly verbatim. Leading with them makes us sound like the sixth entrant in a crowded category. We lead with D-1 and D-2, which nobody else can say.

Why these numbers

  • $8,500 is below the pain threshold and above the toy threshold. It is roughly half of one 510(k) engagement’s research component, so the ROI case closes in a single meeting. It is also 10× the nearest AI-native competitor’s starter tier, which forces a value conversation rather than a price comparison — exactly where D-1 and D-2 win.
  • $22,000 sits deliberately under the incumbent eQMS median of $43,989. We are a second line item in a budget that already has an eQMS in it. Being visibly cheaper than the eQMS makes that easier, and the January 2026 price rise widens the gap.
  • The Consultancy tier is priced on leverage. A consultant serving twelve clients gets twelve isolated workspaces for $29,000 — under $2,500 per client against $17,500–$50,000 of engagement value each. Highest margin, highest referral, and it is the SKU we build first.
  • Meter the artefact, not the query. Punitive metering on a research tool destroys the exploration behaviour that creates the habit. We cap reports; we never cap curiosity.

Land and expand

  • Land on predicate discovery. It is the most acutely painful manual task and the clearest proof the data layer works. It is also the fastest demo: describe a device, get ranked predicates with an indications-for-use diff, in under fifteen minutes.
  • Expand into cost and timeline modelling. This brings a second stakeholder — finance, or the founder — into the account and raises the budget ceiling.
  • Retain with monitoring and change assessment. This is the difference between a tool bought for a project and a subscription renewed for years.

The structural risk, stated plainly. This product is bought for a project, and the project ends. A team that has filed has no reason to open the tool again for eighteen months. Without the watchlist, alerting and change-assessment hooks, Year 2 churn will be severe regardless of how good the analysis is. Those features are revenue-retention requirements wearing a feature’s clothing, they ship by month 12 and month 14 respectively, and we track cohort retention from the first pilot rather than from general availability.

Market expansion after the baseline

The baseline is the United States, and nothing else. Scope then grows outward in phases, cheapest-and-nearest first, each one gated on the commercial health of the market before it. The data model is jurisdiction-parameterised from day one, which is what makes this ladder affordable rather than aspirational.

The expansion gate. No second jurisdiction opens until the US business shows net revenue retention ≥ 110%, annual logo churn < 12%, and consultant-sourced pipeline ≥ 30%. The temptation at month 12 will be to announce a second geography, because it is a better slide than a retention number. We have written the gate down in advance so that the decision is made on evidence. A vertical product that expands geographically before it has retained its first market is not expanding — it is carrying a retention problem into a second market.

If it fails, extraction is restricted to post-2010 summaries — ~60% of the corpus and ~85% of what users actually cite — and the limitation is stated in the product. Terms

Instrument and valuation are open for discussion. We are indifferent between a priced round and a SAFE, and would rather optimise for the right investors than for the last basis point of a cap. What we are not flexible on is the size: below $1M the go-to-market line gets cut first, and a product with no evidence of demand at month 18 is not a fundable company — it is an expensive prototype.

The one-line thesis. Own the price band nobody serves, with the one property nobody claims: every determination reproducible against a dated snapshot of the public record. Sell it first to the people whose job is to defend the answer — consultants and small regulatory teams — and let their clients follow.
